# Activity 7: Participation and Partnership Recovery Plan

**Learning outcome:** LO2  
**Objective:** Remove participation barriers and govern delivery partners for a sustainable programme  
**Expected output:** Participation recovery plan, vendor scorecard and budget-backed experiment  
**Primary source:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK201567/

## Goal

Diagnose low engagement in an existing programme and redesign access, communication, incentives and vendor governance.

## Real-world scenario

The NCBI review highlights multilevel leadership, organisational alignment, programme scope and quality, accessibility, partnerships and communications as core elements, while identifying awareness, time, manager support, access, privacy and perceived benefit as participation barriers.

SkyGarden Retail, a fictional 1,100-person business, pays for an EAP and wellness portal. Registration is 44%, first use 12%, repeat use 3%, and night-shift uptake is below 1%. Leaders propose a prize draw for registrations.

## Evidence boundary

Public case facts and fictional learner data must never be mixed. Every numerical or organisational training input is labelled as an assumption.

### Training assumptions / guardrails

- All funnel, budget, vendor and employee-comment data are fictional.
- Registration is not evidence of benefit and may be a vanity metric.
- The vendor can provide privacy-safe aggregates but the current contract lacks response-time and accessibility standards.
- A S$35,000 budget can be reallocated across access, communication, manager release time and service capacity.

## Questions

1. Where does the participation funnel fail for each workforce group?
2. Which barriers are awareness, access, trust, relevance, manager or service-quality problems?
3. Would the proposed incentive support or distort the intended behaviour?
4. What partner standards and experiment would restore sustainable participation?

## Detailed activity procedure

### Step 1: Analyse the funnel

Calculate eligible-to-aware, aware-to-register, register-to-first-use and first-to-repeat conversion by group. Apply small-cell suppression.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 2: Code employee voice

Group comments into awareness, time, access, trust, relevance, manager and service experience. Preserve contradictory evidence.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 3: Find root causes

Use a barrier map to distinguish communication symptoms from work-design, service-capacity, privacy and vendor causes.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 4: Review the incentive

Test voluntariness, fairness, behaviour targeted, privacy and sustainability. Redesign or reject the prize draw with reasons.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 5: Strengthen the partnership

Add service standards for response, quality, escalation, accessibility, safe aggregates, security, breach handling and corrective action.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 6: Reallocate budget

Fund changes across paid access time, shift coverage, trusted communication, language/accessibility, service capacity and evaluation.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 7: Design a recovery experiment

State target group, barrier, change, baseline, measure, duration, safeguard and success/stop rule.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 8: Set governance

Assign owner, vendor forum, issue route, monthly evidence review and decision date for adapt, scale or exit.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

## Deliverable

Participation recovery plan, vendor scorecard and budget-backed experiment

## Acceptance criteria

The plan diagnoses funnel loss by group, addresses at least three root-cause categories, avoids coercive incentives, sets vendor quality/privacy standards and defines a measurable experiment with owner and stop rule.

## Reflection and debrief questions

1. Which assumption changed your recommendation most, and why?
2. What evidence would you request before relying on the result?
3. Which stakeholder owns the next decision or mitigation?
4. Which review trigger or future exercise should follow?
