# Activity 8: Inclusive Multi-Channel Wellbeing Campaign

**Learning outcome:** LO3  
**Objective:** Communicate credible programme benefits through appropriate techniques and channels  
**Expected output:** Audience-message-channel matrix and six-week campaign plan  
**Primary source:** https://www.gallup.com/workplace/404105/importance-of-employee-wellbeing.aspx

## Goal

Design a trust-centred campaign that reaches office, frontline and mobile employees without overclaiming outcomes.

## Real-world scenario

Gallup's research treats career wellbeing as foundational and describes employee wellbeing as connected with engagement, absence, performance and retention. These relationships can guide a value proposition but do not prove that a single campaign or programme causes each outcome.

MetroCare Facilities, a fictional employer, is launching a whole-person support hub. Past email-only campaigns reached office staff but missed cleaners, technicians and overnight teams. Employees worry that managers can see EAP usage.

## Evidence boundary

Public case facts and fictional learner data must never be mixed. Every numerical or organisational training input is labelled as an assumption.

### Training assumptions / guardrails

- Personas, channel reach, trust scores and message-test results are fictional.
- EAP individual use is confidential to the provider, subject to stated safety and legal boundaries.
- The campaign has six weeks and a S$18,000 budget.
- No message may promise improved health, productivity or promotion outcomes to an individual participant.

## Questions

1. What behaviour or decision should each audience take?
2. Which benefit, barrier, messenger and channel combination fits each group?
3. How will the campaign explain privacy and voluntariness accurately?
4. Which measures distinguish delivery, comprehension, trust and action?

## Detailed activity procedure

### Step 1: Define campaign outcomes

Set one awareness, one comprehension and one access behaviour per priority audience. Avoid vague goals such as raise awareness.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 2: Segment by access need

Use role, schedule, location, language and channel access rather than unsupported demographic stereotypes.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 3: Map barriers and motivations

For each audience, record time, trust, relevance, manager, digital and service barriers plus a credible employee benefit.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 4: Write the message architecture

Create Why–What–How–Trust copy with accurate eligibility, provider, privacy boundary, action and alternative route.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 5: Select channels and messengers

Combine digital, physical, manager, peer and service-moment channels. Add paid time or roster access where communication alone cannot solve reach.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 6: Prototype and test

Run comprehension and trust testing with diverse employees. Record misinterpretations and revise before launch.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 7: Plan cadence

Sequence pre-launch trust, launch action, manager reinforcement and reminder messages without fatigue. Keep details consistent across channels.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 8: Measure and adapt

Track reach, comprehension, trust, access attempts and equitable uptake. Define weekly corrections and a post-campaign review.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

## Deliverable

Audience-message-channel matrix and six-week campaign plan

## Acceptance criteria

Every priority audience has a defined action, credible benefit, trusted messenger and accessible channel; privacy is accurate; overclaiming is absent; and measures cover reach, comprehension, trust, action and equity.

## Reflection and debrief questions

1. Which assumption changed your recommendation most, and why?
2. What evidence would you request before relying on the result?
3. Which stakeholder owns the next decision or mitigation?
4. Which review trigger or future exercise should follow?
