# Activity 2: Segmented Workforce Wellbeing Needs Assessment

**Learning outcome:** LO2  
**Objective:** Identify the current health and wellness state of different employee groups using privacy-safe evidence  
**Expected output:** Needs-assessment matrix and a two-priority management brief  
**Primary source:** https://www.gallup.com/workplace/404105/importance-of-employee-wellbeing.aspx

## Goal

Combine workforce voice and operational signals to prioritise needs without diagnosing employees or exposing small groups.

## Real-world scenario

Gallup frames wellbeing through career, social, financial, physical and community elements and links the employee experience to outcomes such as engagement, absence and retention. The framework is a lens for inquiry, not a diagnostic instrument.

NorthQuay Services, a fictional 420-person firm, has a head office, a 24/7 contact centre and mobile technicians. Leaders want one annual wellness event, while employee feedback suggests different barriers by group.

## Evidence boundary

Public case facts and fictional learner data must never be mixed. Every numerical or organisational training input is labelled as an assumption.

### Training assumptions / guardrails

- All survey, absence, overtime, turnover and focus-group data in the activity pack are fictional.
- Groups with fewer than ten responses must not be reported separately.
- Operational indicators have multiple possible causes and cannot diagnose health conditions.
- The training dataset intentionally includes missing responses and unequal response rates.

## Questions

1. What can each evidence source support, and what can it not support?
2. Which needs are shared and which differ by workforce group?
3. Where could response bias, small numbers or stereotyping distort the result?
4. Which two priorities should management address first, and why?

## Detailed activity procedure

### Step 1: Define the management decision

State the programme decision, time horizon and employee groups. Do not begin by choosing an intervention.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 2: Create an evidence inventory

List survey, focus group, operational, EAP and risk data. Record source, period, coverage, privacy risk and known limitation.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 3: Check data quality

Calculate response and missing-data rates. Flag small cells, inconsistent denominators, outliers and groups that are not adequately represented.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 4: Describe each segment

Summarise wellbeing conditions and barriers for office, contact-centre and mobile employees using neutral, non-diagnostic language.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 5: Triangulate

Require at least two independent signals before treating a need as established. Record contradictions rather than hiding them.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 6: Apply an equity lens

Compare access, job demands, control, schedule, manager support and service reach. Avoid attributing group differences to personal weakness.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 7: Prioritise transparently

Score materiality, employee voice, modifiability, equity, urgency and evidence confidence. Select two priorities and one research gap.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

### Step 8: Prepare the management brief

Present the current-state matrix, limitations, priority rationale, next evidence action and privacy-safe reporting rules.

**Checkpoint:** Record the evidence, calculation or decision produced in this step.

## Deliverable

Needs-assessment matrix and a two-priority management brief

## Acceptance criteria

Every priority uses at least two evidence signals, small groups are suppressed, uncertainty is explicit, no condition is diagnosed, and recommended action addresses a workplace factor plus an access need.

## Reflection and debrief questions

1. Which assumption changed your recommendation most, and why?
2. What evidence would you request before relying on the result?
3. Which stakeholder owns the next decision or mitigation?
4. Which review trigger or future exercise should follow?
